Creative Projects

Here are some of the creative projects I've worked on over the years. From a young age, I have loved designing experiences for people. Whether it be hosting immersive parties, special effects in YouTube videos, or working as a haunted house scare actor, nothing brings me as much joy as being the orchestrator of gripping sensory stimuli.

Oculizer

Over the course of two years, I've developed an advanced DMX lighting automation system that creates real-time, music-reactive light shows. Oculizer combines Spotify metadata with live audio analysis, using mel-scaled FFT to analyze frequency components and map them to DMX values through configurable scenes.

My hope is that this project is the precursor to developing systems that facilitate fruitful social interactions. Our brains are highly sensitive to the spectra of ambient light, sound, and odor in the environment, but the ways in which these factors impact our feelings and behavior often evades conscious awareness. Through a systematic research program of testing the relationships between these factors and human behavior, I hope to help build the knowledge necessary for us to optimize the spaces we inhabit towards connection and cooperation.

Core Features

  • Real-time audio reactivity using mel-scaled FFT analysis
  • Spotify integration for metadata and playback control
  • Support for RGB lights, dimmers, strobes, and lasers
  • Live scene switching and MIDI control support

The Art of Conversation

Funded by an arts integration grant, I worked with my colleagues in the SCRAP Lab to build an augmented social experience that was featured at the Hopkins Center for the Arts.

Our installation, title "The Art of Conversation", was an enviornment that stacked time on top of itself. Pairs of participants viewed gallery art together while having the semantic content of their conversations analyzed in real-time. The words they spoke were used to index utterances from all previous conversations that were similar in meaning and played back to them in real time. This created the effect where each spoken sentence triggered similar sentences from other conversations.

From the artistic side, our goal was to create a space where participants could see themselves in the context of the larger collective conversation - to reveal the forest to the trees. On the scientific side, we are using this data to understand how people's conversations are shaped by the conversations of others, and how the meaning of words is influenced by the context in which they are spoken.
