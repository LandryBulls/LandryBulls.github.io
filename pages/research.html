Understanding Social Emergence

How do our brains navigate the complexity of multiple people at once?
How do the trees experience the forest?

Research Overview

I'm interested in the hidden dynamics of complex social interactions like group conversations, dance floors, and rituals. What is the nature of self within these dynamic, ever-changing social environments that blur the boundaries between one individual and the next?

My research explores how the brain processes and integrates the massive amount of social information available in face-to-face group interactions. I'm particularly interested in:

  • How bodily gestures, facial expressions, word patterns, and vocal intonations combine in multi-person conversations
  • The computational principles underlying how the social brain synthesizes information about multiple people
  • Neural representations of groups and their emergent properties
  • How multimodal signatures in face-to-face interactions index the health and vitality of social systems

The Challenge of Social Participation

Imagine a group of friends at a dinner party, a team of security personnel responding to a threat, or the fluid-like undulations of a densely-packed concert audience. How do individuals in these groups square their individual behaviors with the perceived whole in which they participate? How does joining a crowd shape our individual perspectives and behaviors? What does participation really mean in each of these scenarios, and what are the social cues that enable it?

Using cutting-edge methods from computer science, social psychology, and neuroscience, I'm uncovering the hidden patterns that determine when groups "click" and when they don't.

Approach

Multi-Modal Recording

Capturing natural conversations and cultural events from multiple angles using synchronized audiovisual recordings.

Artificial Neural Networks

Using deep learning to extract subtle patterns in body language, speech, and group synchrony.

Dynamical Systems

Modeling social interactions as complex systems to understand how the pulse of social life emerges from individual actions.

Key Findings

Questions facilitate connection

In group conversations, people feel more connected to those who ask more questions.

Impact

Applications

  • Improving team dynamics in workplace settings
  • Developing more natural social robotics
  • Supporting group therapy and counseling
  • Understanding collective decision making

Future Directions

We're working to develop real-time tools that can help facilitate better group interactions, from business meetings to social gatherings.
